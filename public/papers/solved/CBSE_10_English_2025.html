📖 Section A: Reading Comprehension (20 Marks)

Is section mein do unseen passages hain — ek factual/discursive aur ek note making/headings wala.

Passage 1 10 Marks
[Unseen Passage - Factual/Discursive]

Read the following passage carefully and answer the questions that follow:

The rapid advancement of technology has transformed every aspect of our lives. From the way we communicate to how we learn, technology has become an integral part of our daily existence. In the field of education, digital tools have opened new avenues for students, making learning more interactive and accessible. However, this digital revolution also brings challenges such as screen addiction, cyberbullying, and the digital divide between those who have access to technology and those who do not.
Q1. How has technology transformed education? (2 Marks)
✅ Answer / Uttar
Technology has transformed education by making learning more interactive and accessible. Digital tools have opened new avenues for students, allowing them to access information easily and learn through multimedia content.
Q2. What challenges does the digital revolution bring? (2 Marks)
✅ Answer / Uttar
The digital revolution brings challenges such as screen addiction, cyberbullying, and the digital divide — the gap between those who have access to technology and those who do not.
Q3. Find the word from the passage which means 'essential'. (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
Integral — The passage states "technology has become an integral part of our daily existence."
Q4. What is meant by 'digital divide'? (2 Marks)
✅ Answer / Uttar
The digital divide refers to the gap or inequality between people who have access to modern technology (computers, internet) and those who do not have such access, creating unequal opportunities in education and development.

Q1. Formal Letter 5 Marks
You are Rahul/Rita, a resident of 45, Green Park, New Delhi. Write a letter to the Editor of a national daily expressing your concern about the increasing pollution in your city and suggesting measures to control it.
✅ Answer / Uttar
45, Green Park,
New Delhi - 110016
20th March 2025

The Editor,
The Times of India,
New Delhi

Subject: Concern Regarding Increasing Pollution in Delhi

Sir/Madam,

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities and the general public towards the alarming rise in pollution levels in our city.

Delhi has been grappling with severe air pollution for the past few years. The Air Quality Index (AQI) frequently crosses the hazardous mark, putting the health of millions at risk. The major causes include vehicular emissions, construction dust, industrial waste, and stubble burning in neighbouring states.

This pollution leads to serious respiratory diseases, especially among children and the elderly. It also reduces visibility, causing accidents and disrupting daily life.

To tackle this menace, the government should strictly enforce pollution control norms, promote public transportation, and increase green cover. As citizens, we must carpool, use public transport, and plant more trees. Banning plastic and proper waste management can also contribute significantly.

I hope the concerned authorities will take immediate action to save our city from this environmental disaster.

Yours faithfully,
Rahul Sharma
Q2. Analytical Paragraph 5 Marks
Write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words on the given pie chart showing the distribution of time spent by students on different activities in a day.
✅ Answer / Uttar
The given pie chart illustrates how students distribute their time among various daily activities. Studying occupies the largest portion at 35%, indicating that academics remain the primary focus. Sleep takes up 25%, which is essential for physical and mental health. Recreation and entertainment account for 15%, showing that students do allocate time for relaxation. Physical activities and sports consume 10%, while social media usage also takes 10%. The remaining 5% is spent on miscellaneous activities. Overall, the chart reveals a balanced lifestyle, though the equal time for sports and social media raises concerns about digital addiction. Students should ideally increase physical activity time and reduce screen time for better health.
📝 Section B (contd.): Grammar (10 Marks) — Writing + Grammar = 20 Marks total

Grammar section mein editing, gap filling, transformation of sentences aur reported speech aate hain.

Q1. Gap Filling (Tenses) 3 Marks
Fill in the blanks with correct form of verbs given in brackets:
(a) She ______ (work) in this company since 2020.
(b) By the time we arrived, the film ______ (already/start).
(c) If I ______ (be) you, I would accept the offer.
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) She has been working in this company since 2020. (Present Perfect Continuous — action started in past and continues)
(b) By the time we arrived, the film had already started. (Past Perfect — action completed before another past action)
(c) If I were you, I would accept the offer. (Subjunctive mood — hypothetical situation)

Q3. Reported Speech 4 Marks
Change the following sentences into indirect speech:
(a) The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
(b) He said to me, "Have you completed your homework?"
(c) She said, "I will call you tomorrow."
(d) The beggar said, "Please give me some food."
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun. (Universal truth — tense doesn't change)
(b) He asked me if/whether I had completed my homework. (Yes/No question — 'if/whether' + past perfect)
(c) She said that she would call me the next day. (Will → would, tomorrow → next day)
(d) The beggar requested me to give him some food. (Imperative — requested + to + verb)
📚 Section C: Literature (40 Marks)

Literature section mein First Flight (Prose + Poem) aur Footprints Without Feet (Supplementary) se questions aate hain. Extract-based, short answer aur long answer hote hain.

Extract 1: Two Stories About Flying 5 Marks
"The moon was coming up in the east, behind me, and stars were shining in the clear sky above me. There wasn't a cloud in the sky. I was happy to be alone high up above the sleeping countryside."
(a) Who is the speaker in this extract? (1 Mark)
(b) What time of the day is described here? (1 Mark)
(c) Why was the narrator happy? (2 Marks)
(d) Find a word from the extract which means 'land outside towns and cities'. (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) The speaker is the young seagull (from "His First Flight" by Liam O'Flaherty).

(b) It is night time — described by the moon coming up and stars shining.

(c) The narrator was happy because he was flying alone high above the sleeping countryside. He had overcome his fear of flying and was experiencing the joy and freedom of flight for the first time.

(d) Countryside — means land outside towns and cities.
Extract 2: Nelson Mandela 5 Marks
"No one is born hating another person because of the colour of his skin, or his background, or his religion. People must learn to hate, and if they can learn to hate, they can be taught to love, for love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite."
(a) Who said these words and on what occasion? (2 Marks)
(b) What does the speaker mean by 'love comes more naturally'? (2 Marks)
(c) What is the 'opposite' of love referred to here? (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) These words were spoken by Nelson Mandela in his autobiography "Long Walk to Freedom". He said this while reflecting on his inauguration as South Africa's first black President and his views on humanity and reconciliation.

(b) By 'love comes more naturally', Mandela means that love, tolerance, and acceptance are innate human qualities. Hatred is not natural; it is learned through social conditioning. If people can be taught to hate, they can equally be taught to love, as love is the more fundamental human instinct.

(c) The 'opposite' of love referred to here is hate.
Short Answer: The Thief's Story 3 Marks
Why did Hari Singh decide to return the stolen money to Anil?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Hari Singh decided to return the stolen money to Anil because:

1. Anil's trust and kindness deeply affected him. Anil had taught him to read and write and treated him with warmth, which made Hari feel guilty.

2. He realized that education and honest living were more valuable than stolen money. Anil was giving him a chance to change his life.

3. He understood that trust is more precious than money — if he lost Anil's trust, he would lose the only person who believed in him.
Long Answer: A Letter to God 5 Marks
Describe Lencho's faith in God. How did the postmaster help him? What does this story teach us about human nature?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Lencho's Faith in God:
Lencho was a simple farmer who had unshakeable faith in God. He believed that God could see everything and would never let his innocent children starve. When his crops were destroyed by hailstones, he didn't lose hope. Instead, he wrote a direct letter to God asking for 100 pesos to sow his field again. This shows his childlike, pure faith — he believed God would personally help him.

Postmaster's Help:
The postmaster was deeply moved by Lencho's faith. To preserve this faith, he collected money from his employees and friends and sent 70 pesos to Lencho in God's name. Though he couldn't collect the full amount, his act of kindness showed humanity and compassion.

Lesson about Human Nature:
The story teaches us that faith can move mountains. It also shows the contrast between pure faith (Lencho) and human kindness (postmaster). Ironically, Lencho called the post office employees 'a bunch of crooks' for stealing 30 pesos, not knowing they were the ones who helped him. This highlights how humans often doubt others' intentions while maintaining blind faith in divine power.
Long Answer: Amanda! (Poem) 5 Marks
What is the central theme of the poem "Amanda!" by Robin Klein? How does the poem depict the conflict between parental expectations and a child's freedom?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Central Theme:
The poem "Amanda!" explores the theme of parental nagging and a child's desire for freedom. It shows how constant instructions and restrictions can suffocate a child's imagination and natural spirit.

Conflict Depicted:
1. Parental Expectations: Amanda's mother constantly instructs her — don't bite nails, don't hunch, don't eat chocolate, do homework, clean your room. These represent typical parental concerns about discipline and proper behaviour.

2. Child's Escape: To escape this nagging, Amanda retreats into her imagination — she imagines herself as a mermaid in the emerald sea, an orphan roaming freely, and Rapunzel in a tower. These fantasies represent her desire for freedom, silence, and independence.

3. The Poignant End: The poem ends with the mother asking Amanda if she is listening, while Amanda is lost in her dream world. This shows that constant nagging pushes children away emotionally rather than bringing them closer.

Message: The poem teaches parents to balance guidance with freedom — children need space to dream and grow. Excessive control can create emotional distance and stifle creativity.
Extract 3: The Sermon at Benares 5 Marks
"Kisa Gotami carried her dead child to all her neighbours, asking for medicine, and the people said, 'She has lost her senses. The boy is dead.'"
(a) Why was Kisa Gotami going from house to house? (2 Marks)
(b) What advice did the Buddha give her? (2 Marks)
(c) What did Kisa Gotami finally realize? (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) Kisa Gotami was going from house to house looking for medicine to revive her dead child, refusing to accept that he had died, and hoping someone could bring him back.

(b) The Buddha told her to bring a handful of mustard seeds from a house where no one had ever lost a loved one — a home untouched by death.

(c) Kisa Gotami realized that death is universal — every household had lost someone at some point, and grief is a shared part of the human condition. This helped her accept her child's death and find peace.
Short Answer: Madam Rides the Bus 3 Marks
What was Valli's first impression of the bus journey when she finally took it?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Valli was thrilled and observant during her first bus ride — she watched everything keenly, from the passengers to the scenery outside, and felt a great sense of independence and achievement at having planned and carried out the trip entirely on her own, without any adult's help.
Long Answer: The Tale of Custard the Dragon (Poem) 5 Marks
How does the poem "The Tale of Custard the Dragon" subvert our expectations about courage and cowardice?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Subverting Expectations:
The poem introduces four seemingly brave characters — Belinda, Ink, Blink, and Mustard — who all boast about their courage, while Custard the dragon is mocked as a coward for crying for a nice safe cage.

The Twist:
When a real pirate breaks in, all the supposedly brave characters — Ink, Blink, and Mustard — flee and hide in fear, while Belinda merely screams. It is Custard, the 'coward', who bravely fights and defeats the pirate.

Message:
The poem humorously teaches that true courage is often hidden, and those who boast the loudest about bravery are not always the bravest when real danger strikes. It challenges the reader's assumptions about who is truly courageous.
Short Answer: Glimpses of India 4 Marks
What impression do you form of Goan life from the chapter "Glimpses of India"?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Goan life, as depicted in the chapter, is simple, community-oriented, and deeply tied to local traditions — centred around the daily ritual of fresh bread from the local baker (pader), whose arrival was a cherished part of everyday life, especially for children. The chapter reflects a warm, unhurried way of life rooted in food, festivity, and neighbourly bonds.
