Q2. Analytical Paragraph 5 Marks
Write an analytical paragraph in 120 words on the given bar graph showing the number of books read by students of different classes in a school library.
✅ Answer / Uttar
The given bar graph represents the number of books read by students of different classes in a school library. Class X students are the most avid readers with 150 books, followed by Class IX with 120 books. Class VIII students read 100 books, while Class VII and Class VI read 80 and 60 books respectively. The data reveals a clear trend: reading increases with class/age. Senior students likely have better reading skills and more complex subjects requiring reference books. Additionally, providing age-appropriate books can help increase library engagement among younger students.
📝 Section B (contd.): Grammar (10 Marks) — Writing + Grammar = 20 Marks total
Q1. Gap Filling 3 Marks
(a) She ______ (live) in Mumbai since 2015.
(b) The movie ______ (start) before we reached the theatre.
(c) If I ______ (win) the lottery, I would buy a house.
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) She has been living in Mumbai since 2015. (Present Perfect Continuous — 'since' + point of time)
(b) The movie had started before we reached the theatre. (Past Perfect — action completed before another past action)
(c) If I won the lottery, I would buy a house. (Second conditional — simple past in if-clause)

Q3. Reported Speech 4 Marks
(a) The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east."
(b) My friend said, "Will you help me with this project?"
(c) She said, "I have never seen such a beautiful sunset."
(d) The guard said, "Stop! Who goes there?"
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) The teacher said that the sun rises in the east. (Universal truth — tense remains unchanged)
(b) My friend asked if I would help him with that project. (Yes/No question → if + would, this → that)
(c) She said that she had never seen such a beautiful sunset. (Present perfect → past perfect, such remains)
(d) The guard ordered to stop and asked who went there. (Imperative + question → ordered + asked)
📚 Section C: Literature (40 Marks)
Extract 1: Nelson Mandela 5 Marks
"It was this desire for the freedom of my people to live their lives with dignity and self-respect that animated my life, that transformed a frightened young man into a bold one, that drove a law-abiding attorney to become a criminal, that turned a family-loving husband into a man without a home."
(a) What desire animated Mandela's life? (2 Marks)
(b) How did this desire transform Mandela? (2 Marks)
(c) What does Mandela mean by 'a man without a home'? (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) The desire for the freedom of his people to live with dignity and self-respect animated Mandela's life. He wanted black South Africans to have equal rights and freedom from apartheid.

(b) This desire transformed Mandela in several ways: (i) It turned a frightened young man into a bold one, (ii) It drove a law-abiding attorney to become a criminal (in the eyes of the apartheid government), and (iii) It turned a family-loving husband into a man without a home (as he had to leave his family to fight for freedom).

(c) By 'a man without a home', Mandela means that his dedication to the freedom struggle forced him to leave his family and live in hiding or prison. He could not enjoy a normal family life.
Extract 2: Amanda! 5 Marks
"(I am an orphan, roaming the street.
I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet.
The silence is golden, the freedom is sweet.)"
(a) Why does Amanda imagine herself as an orphan? (2 Marks)
(b) What does 'silence is golden' mean here? (2 Marks)
(c) What is the tone of these lines? (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) Amanda imagines herself as an orphan because she wants freedom from her mother's constant nagging. As an orphan, she would have no parents to instruct or control her. She could roam freely without anyone telling her what to do.

(b) 'Silence is golden' means that silence is precious and valuable to Amanda. At home, her mother constantly speaks to her (instructions, criticism), so she craves the peace and quiet of being alone. For her, silence is more valuable than gold.

(c) The tone is dreamy, wistful, and longing. Amanda is escaping into an imaginary world where she is free and happy, away from the reality of parental control.
Short Answer: The Hack Driver 3 Marks
How did the hack driver (Oliver Lutkins) fool the lawyer?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Oliver Lutkins fooled the lawyer by:

1. Posing as a friendly hack driver: He offered to help the lawyer find Oliver Lutkins, never revealing that he himself was Lutkins.

2. Taking him on a wild goose chase: He took the lawyer to various places — Fritz's shop, Gustaff's barber shop, his mother's farm — pretending to search for Lutkins, but actually just visiting his own acquaintances.

3. Charging money: He earned money from the lawyer as a hack driver while the lawyer paid for his own deception. The lawyer only realized the truth when he returned with a colleague and saw the hack driver being addressed as Lutkins.
Long Answer: The Ball Poem 5 Marks
What is the boy learning in "The Ball Poem"? How does the poet convey the theme of loss through the symbol of the ball?
✅ Answer / Uttar
What the Boy is Learning:
The boy is learning the epistemology of loss — the philosophy and understanding of loss as an inevitable part of life. He is learning that:

1. Loss is inevitable: Everyone loses things, people, and moments in life.

2. He is responsible: The poet says "He is learning, well behind his desperate eyes" — the boy realizes he must bear his own losses and cannot always depend on others to fix things.

3. Moving on: He learns that life goes on despite losses. He must stand up and continue.

Symbol of the Ball:
The ball symbolizes childhood innocence and attachment to material things. When the ball falls into the water, it represents the first experience of loss in the boy's life. The poet emphasizes that "money is external" — a new ball can be bought, but the emotional attachment to the lost ball cannot be replaced. The ball symbolizes how we form attachments to things that are temporary, and learning to let go is part of growing up. The water into which the ball falls represents the unknown future where lost things disappear forever.
Long Answer: The Book That Saved the Earth 5 Marks
How did a book of nursery rhymes save the Earth from the Martian invasion? What does this story tell us about the power of literature?
✅ Answer / Uttar
How the Book Saved Earth:
In the 25th century, the Think-Tank (Mighty Martian) planned to invade Earth. He sent Noodle and other crew members to a library on Earth to understand Earth's defense secrets. They found a book of Mother Goose nursery rhymes.

Think-Tank misinterpreted the rhymes as Earth's secret code:

1. "Mistress Mary, quite contrary" — Think-Tank thought it referred to Earth's growing explosives and weapons.

2. "Hey diddle diddle, the cat and the fiddle" — He interpreted it as Earth's advanced technology (spacecraft shaped like dishes).

3. "Humpty Dumpty had a great fall" — Think-Tank believed this was a threat to Martians, that they would have a great fall like Humpty Dumpty.

Terrified by these "secret messages," Think-Tank called off the invasion and fled to Alpha Centauri.

Power of Literature:
The story humorously shows that literature has the power to influence minds and change outcomes. Even simple nursery rhymes, when misinterpreted, had the power to prevent a war. It shows that knowledge and creativity (even in children's literature) can be more powerful than weapons. The story also satirizes how those in power often misinterpret information due to their own arrogance and fear.

"Tricki was ill. There was no doubt about that... He (Mr. Herriot) resolved to keep the little dog for a while and see what starvation and exercise could do."
(a) What was wrong with Tricki? (2 Marks)
(b) What treatment did the vet decide on? (2 Marks)
(c) Who was Mrs. Pumphrey? (1 Mark)
✅ Answer / Uttar
(a) Tricki was overfed and underexercised by his doting owner, Mrs. Pumphrey — he had become obese, lethargic, and was vomiting.

(b) The vet, Mr. Herriot, decided to keep Tricki away from his owner and put him on a strict regime of starvation (no rich food) and daily exercise with the other farm dogs.

(c) Mrs. Pumphrey was Tricki's wealthy, overindulgent owner who pampered him with rich food and treats, unknowingly making him ill.
Short Answer: The Necklace 3 Marks
Why did Mathilde Loisel borrow the necklace from Madame Forestier?
✅ Answer / Uttar
Mathilde borrowed the necklace because she had no jewellery of her own to wear to the minister's ball and desperately wanted to look rich and elegant. Being dissatisfied with her modest, middle-class life, she wanted to create an impression of wealth and status among the elite guests at the party.
Long Answer: The Midnight Visitor 5 Marks
How did Ausable outwit Max and save the report? Describe the trick he used.
✅ Answer / Uttar
The Situation: Max, a spy, broke into Ausable's room at gunpoint to steal a secret report Ausable was carrying, catching him completely off guard.

Ausable's Trick: Instead of panicking, Ausable calmly told Max a made-up story — that there was no balcony outside his window, and that the French secret police, believing a spy would try to escape through it, had rigged that exact spot as a trap. He claimed anyone stepping onto it would trigger an alarm.

The Outcome: When a knock came at the door (actually just the waiter with sandwiches Ausable had ordered), Max panicked, believing the police had arrived, and jumped out of the window onto the fake 'balcony' to escape — only to fall into the courtyard below, since there was no balcony at all. Ausable's quick thinking and use of psychological deception, rather than violence, saved the report and outwitted the spy.
Short Answer: Fog (Poem) 4 Marks
What does the poet compare fog to in the poem "Fog" by Carl Sandburg? Explain the central image.
✅ Answer / Uttar
The poet Carl Sandburg compares fog to a cat — describing how it "comes on little cat feet", silently and unexpectedly settling over the city, and then moves on just as quietly, "sits looking over harbor and city... and then moves on." The comparison captures how fog arrives without warning, sits quietly for a while observing everything below, and departs suddenly — just like a cat's soft, silent, unpredictable movements. The poem uses this extended metaphor to convey the mysterious and fleeting nature of fog.
