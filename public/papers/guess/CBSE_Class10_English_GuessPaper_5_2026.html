Discursive Passage (10 marks)
Physical fitness is often misunderstood as merely having a muscular body or the ability to run fast. In reality, fitness is a holistic concept that encompasses physical health, mental well-being, and emotional balance. A truly fit person is not just strong but also resilient, focused, and emotionally stable. The benefits of regular physical activity extend far beyond weight management. Studies have consistently shown that exercise improves cognitive function, enhances memory, and reduces the risk of depression and anxiety. When we exercise, our brain releases endorphins — chemicals that create feelings of happiness and reduce pain perception. Sleep is another critical component of fitness that is frequently neglected. In our culture of productivity, sleep is often seen as a waste of time. However, research reveals that during sleep, the brain consolidates memories, removes toxins, and repairs tissues. Adults need 7-9 hours of quality sleep, yet a staggering 35% of adults report getting less than 7 hours regularly. Nutrition completes the fitness triangle. The food we consume is the fuel for our body and mind. A balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins provides the nutrients necessary for optimal functioning. Unfortunately, the convenience of processed foods has led to a global epidemic of poor nutrition.

Q9(a)6 marks
Describe Maxwell's experience of keeping an otter as a pet. What does this story teach us about the relationship between humans and animals? (100-120 words)
Answer: Gavin Maxwell's experience with Mijbil was both challenging and rewarding. He acquired Mijbil in Iraq and brought him to London. Initially frightened and aloof, Mijbil gradually developed a strong bond with Maxwell, playing in the bathroom, turning on taps, and lying on his bed. The traumatic flight to London tested this bond, but Maxwell's patience helped Mijbil adjust. The story shows how animals respond to kindness rather than control — Maxwell never dominated Mijbil, giving him freedom to be himself. This teaches that human-animal relationships should be based on mutual respect and trust, not control, and that animals given love and space become loyal, affectionate companions.Maxwell aur Mijbil ki kahani sikhati hai ki human-animal relationship respect aur trust pe based hona chahiye.
Q9(b)6 marks
"Sometimes we need to be pushed to discover our own strength." Discuss with reference to the young seagull's story. (100-120 words)
Answer: The young seagull's story powerfully illustrates this idea. Despite having fully developed wings, he refused to fly, paralysed by fear of the vast sea below. His parents' seemingly harsh methods — taunting, starving him, tempting him just out of reach — were, in fact, necessary pushes. Left to his own comfort, the seagull might never have discovered his innate ability to fly. It was only when hunger overcame fear that he finally dived and realised his wings could hold him all along. This teaches us that we often underestimate our own capabilities until circumstances or people who care about us push us beyond our self-imposed limits.Young seagull ka darr tha, par parents ke tough love ne usse push kiya. Kabhi kabhi push milne se hi apni strength pata chalti hai.
Long Answer Question — Footprints Without Feet (6 marks)
Attempt any 1 out of the following 2 questions. 100-120 words. 6 marks.
Q10(a)6 marks
How did Horace Danby's habit of stealing once a year lead to his downfall? What does this story teach us about honesty and deception? (100-120 words)
Answer: Horace Danby, a respectable locksmith, stole once a year to buy rare books, planning his thefts meticulously. His downfall came when he broke into Shotover Grange and met a young woman pretending to be the owner's wife. Trusting her story, he even opened the safe for her to 'retrieve her jewels' — she was actually a thief who escaped, leaving his fingerprints everywhere, and he was arrested. This story teaches that dishonesty, even in small doses, eventually catches up with us. It also shows deception begets deception — she deceived him just as he had deceived society. Honesty is the only sustainable path, and trust should never be given blindly.Horace saal mein ek baar chori karta tha. Ek lady ne usse bewakoof banaya aur woh pakda gaya.
Q10(b)6 marks
Discuss the character of Mrs. Pumphrey. How does the story use her relationship with Tricki to comment on human behaviour? (100-120 words)
Answer: Mrs. Pumphrey is a wealthy, well-meaning but excessively indulgent dog owner who equates love with overfeeding. Her constant treats make Tricki dangerously obese and lethargic. When Dr. Herriot places Tricki on strict discipline — controlled diet and exercise — he transforms into a healthy dog, ironically becoming greedy for other dogs' food at the surgery. The story uses Mrs. Pumphrey to satirise how misguided affection, when it avoids necessary discipline, can harm rather than help — a lesson equally applicable to parenting and any close relationship.Mrs. Pumphrey pyar ko overfeeding samajhti hai. Story dikhati hai ki discipline ke bina pyar nuksaan pahuncha sakta hai.
