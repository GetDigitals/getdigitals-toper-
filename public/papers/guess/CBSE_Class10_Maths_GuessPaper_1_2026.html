Section A – Multiple Choice Questions 20 × 1 = 20 Marks
1 1 Mark

The HCF of 96 and 404 is:

  • (a) 4
  • (b) 8
  • (c) 12
  • (d) 16
✅ Answer: (a) 4
Using Euclid's Division Algorithm: 404 = 96 × 4 + 20 96 = 20 × 4 + 16 20 = 16 × 1 + 4 16 = 4 × 4 + 0 ∴ HCF = 4
2 1 Mark

If one zero of the polynomial p(x) = x² – 5x + k is 2, then the value of k is:

  • (a) 6
  • (b) –6
  • (c) 3
  • (d) –3
✅ Answer: (a) 6
Since 2 is a zero, p(2) = 0 (2)² – 5(2) + k = 0 4 – 10 + k = 0 k = 6
3 1 Mark

The pair of equations 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 10 is:

  • (a) Consistent with unique solution
  • (b) Consistent with infinitely many solutions
  • (c) Inconsistent
  • (d) None of these
✅ Answer: (b) Consistent with infinitely many solutions
a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2 b₁/b₂ = 3/6 = 1/2 c₁/c₂ = 5/10 = 1/2 Since a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂, equations represent coincident lines. ∴ Infinitely many solutions.
4 1 Mark

The quadratic equation 2x² – 3x + 5 = 0 has:

  • (a) Two distinct real roots
  • (b) Two equal real roots
  • (c) No real roots
  • (d) More than 2 real roots
✅ Answer: (c) No real roots
Discriminant D = b² – 4ac D = (–3)² – 4(2)(5) = 9 – 40 = –31 Since D < 0, no real roots.

a = 2, d = 7 – 2 = 5 a₁₀ = a + 9d = 2 + 9(5) = 2 + 45 = 47

In ΔABC, DE || BC. If AD = 2 cm, DB = 3 cm and AE = 3.2 cm, then EC = ?

  • (a) 4.8 cm

  • (c) 4.2 cm

7 1 Mark

The distance between the points (3, 4) and (–3, –4) is:

  • (a) 8 units
  • (b) 10 units
  • (c) 12 units
  • (d) 14 units
✅ Answer: (b) 10 units
Distance = √[(x₂–x₁)² + (y₂–y₁)²] = √[(–3–3)² + (–4–4)²] = √[(–6)² + (–8)²] = √[36 + 64] = √100 = 10 units
8 1 Mark

If sin θ = 3/5, then cos θ = ?

  • (a) 4/5
  • (b) 3/4
  • (c) 5/4
  • (d) 5/3
✅ Answer: (a) 4/5
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 cos²θ = 1 – (3/5)² = 1 – 9/25 = 16/25 cos θ = 4/5
9 1 Mark

A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at a point Q so that OQ = 13 cm. Length PQ is:

  • (a) 12 cm
  • (b) 13 cm
  • (c) 8 cm
  • (d) 10 cm
✅ Answer: (a) 12 cm
Since tangent ⊥ radius, ∠OPQ = 90° In right ΔOPQ: PQ² = OQ² – OP² = 13² – 5² = 169 – 25 = 144 PQ = 12 cm

Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr² = (60/360) × 3.14 × 6² = (1/6) × 3.14 × 36 = 3.14 × 6 = 18.84 cm²

Assertion (A): The decimal expansion of 17/8 terminates.
Reason (R): A rational number p/q has terminating decimal expansion if prime factors of q are of the form 2ⁿ × 5ᵐ.

  • (a) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation of A
  • (b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT correct explanation of A
  • (c) A is true but R is false
  • (d) A is false but R is true
✅ Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation of A
17/8 = 17/2³ = 2.125 (terminates) q = 8 = 2³, which is of form 2ⁿ × 5ᵐ (n=3, m=0) ∴ Both A and R are true and R explains A.
15 1 Mark

If the sum of first n terms of an AP is Sₙ = 3n² + 5n, then its common difference is:

  • (a) 3
  • (b) 5
  • (c) 6
  • (d) 8
✅ Answer: (c) 6
S₁ = a₁ = 3(1)² + 5(1) = 8 S₂ = 3(4) + 10 = 22 a₂ = S₂ – S₁ = 22 – 8 = 14 d = a₂ – a₁ = 14 – 8 = 6
16 1 Mark

In a right triangle ABC, right-angled at B, if tan A = 1/√3, then sin A cos C + cos A sin C = ?

  • (a) 0
  • (b) 1
  • (c) 1/2
  • (d) √3/2
✅ Answer: (b) 1
tan A = 1/√3 ⇒ A = 30° Since C = 90° – 30° = 60° sin A cos C + cos A sin C = sin(A+C) = sin(90°) = 1

The ratio of the volumes of two spheres is 8 : 27. The ratio of their surface areas is:

  • (a) 2 : 3

V₁/V₂ = (r₁/r₂)³ = 8/27 r₁/r₂ = 2/3 Surface area ratio = (r₁/r₂)² = (2/3)² = 4/9

23 2 Marks

Solve: 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x – 4y = –24. Hence find 'm' if y = mx + 3.

✅ Solution:
Eq (1): 2x + 3y = 11 Eq (2): 2x – 4y = –24 Subtract (2) from (1): 7y = 35 ⇒ y = 5 From (1): 2x + 15 = 11 ⇒ 2x = –4 ⇒ x = –2 Now y = mx + 3 5 = m(–2) + 3 5 – 3 = –2m 2 = –2m ⇒ m = –1
24 2 Marks

In an AP, if a = 7, aₙ = 84 and Sₙ = 455, find n and d.

✅ Solution:
Sₙ = n/2 (a + aₙ) 455 = n/2 (7 + 84) 455 = n/2 × 91 n = (455 × 2)/91 = 910/91 = 10 Now aₙ = a + (n–1)d 84 = 7 + 9d 77 = 9d ⇒ d = 77/9
25 2 Marks

Prove that √3 is irrational.

✅ Solution:
Let us assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are co-prime integers and q ≠ 0. Squaring both sides: 3 = p²/q² ⇒ p² = 3q² ∴ p² is divisible by 3 ⇒ p is divisible by 3 Let p = 3k (3k)² = 3q² ⇒ 9k² = 3q² ⇒ q² = 3k² ∴ q² is divisible by 3 ⇒ q is divisible by 3 This contradicts that p and q are co-prime. Hence, √3 is irrational. (Proved)

26 3 Marks

Find the zeroes of the polynomial 6x² – 7x – 3 and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients.

✅ Solution:
6x² – 7x – 3 = 0 6x² – 9x + 2x – 3 = 0 3x(2x – 3) + 1(2x – 3) = 0 (3x + 1)(2x – 3) = 0 x = –1/3 or x = 3/2 Verification: Sum of zeroes = –1/3 + 3/2 = (–2 + 9)/6 = 7/6 = –(–7)/6 = –b/a ✓ Product of zeroes = (–1/3)(3/2) = –3/6 = –1/2 = c/a ✓
27 3 Marks

A fraction becomes 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from the numerator and it becomes 1/4 when 8 is added to its denominator. Find the fraction.

✅ Solution:
Let fraction = x/y Condition 1: (x–1)/y = 1/3 ⇒ 3x – 3 = y ⇒ 3x – y = 3 ... (i) Condition 2: x/(y+8) = 1/4 ⇒ 4x = y + 8 ⇒ 4x – y = 8 ... (ii) Subtract (i) from (ii): x = 5 From (i): 3(5) – y = 3 ⇒ 15 – y = 3 ⇒ y = 12 ∴ Fraction = 5/12
28 3 Marks

Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

✅ Solution:
Given: A circle with centre O and tangent XY at point P. To Prove: OP ⊥ XY Construction: Take any point Q (≠ P) on XY. Join OQ. Proof: Q lies on tangent XY and outside the circle. ∴ OQ > OP (radius is shortest distance from centre to tangent) This is true for every point Q on XY except P. ∴ OP is the shortest distance from O to XY. Hence, OP ⊥ XY. (Proved)
29 3 Marks

If sec 4A = cosec (A – 20°), where 4A is an acute angle, find the value of A.

✅ Solution:
sec 4A = cosec (A – 20°) cosec (90° – 4A) = cosec (A – 20°) 90° – 4A = A – 20° 90° + 20° = A + 4A 110° = 5A A = 22°
30 3 Marks

Find the area of the shaded region in the figure where ABCD is a square of side 14 cm and four circles are inscribed in it touching each other.

✅ Solution:
Side of square = 14 cm Diameter of each circle = 14/2 = 7 cm Radius of each circle = 3.5 cm Area of square = 14 × 14 = 196 cm² Area of 4 circles = 4 × πr² = 4 × (22/7) × 3.5 × 3.5 = 4 × 22 × 0.5 × 3.5 = 4 × 38.5 = 154 cm² Shaded area = 196 – 154 = 42 cm²

The following distribution shows the daily pocket allowance of children of a locality. The mean pocket allowance is ₹18. Find the missing frequency f.

Class: 11-13, 13-15, 15-17, 17-19, 19-21, 21-23, 23-25
Frequency: 7, 6, 9, 13, f, 5, 4

✅ Solution:
Class marks (x): 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24 Let A = 18 (assumed mean) dᵢ = xᵢ – 18: –6, –4, –2, 0, 2, 4, 6 fᵢdᵢ: –42, –24, –18, 0, 2f, 20, 24 Σf = 7+6+9+13+f+5+4 = 44 + f Σfd = –42–24–18+0+2f+20+24 = –40 + 2f Mean = A + Σfd/Σf 18 = 18 + (–40 + 2f)/(44 + f) 0 = (–40 + 2f)/(44 + f) –40 + 2f = 0 f = 20

32 5 Marks

A motor boat whose speed is 18 km/h in still water takes 1 hour more to go 24 km upstream than to return downstream to the same spot. Find the speed of the stream.

✅ Solution:
Let speed of stream = x km/h Speed upstream = (18 – x) km/h Speed downstream = (18 + x) km/h Time upstream = 24/(18 – x) Time downstream = 24/(18 + x) Given: Time upstream – Time downstream = 1 24/(18 – x) – 24/(18 + x) = 1 24[(18+x) – (18–x)] / [(18–x)(18+x)] = 1 24(2x) / (324 – x²) = 1 48x = 324 – x² x² + 48x – 324 = 0 x² + 54x – 6x – 324 = 0 x(x + 54) – 6(x + 54) = 0 (x – 6)(x + 54) = 0 x = 6 or x = –54 (rejected, speed can't be negative) ∴ Speed of stream = 6 km/h
33 5 Marks

In ΔABC, altitudes AD and CE intersect each other at point P. Prove that:
(i) ΔAEP ~ ΔCDP
(ii) ΔABD ~ ΔCBE
(iii) ΔAEP ~ ΔADB

✅ Solution:
Given: AD ⊥ BC and CE ⊥ AB. AD and CE intersect at P. (i) In ΔAEP and ΔCDP: ∠AEP = ∠CDP = 90° (altitudes) ∠APE = ∠CPD (vertically opposite angles) ∴ ΔAEP ~ ΔCDP (by AA similarity criterion) (ii) In ΔABD and ΔCBE: ∠ADB = ∠CEB = 90° (altitudes) ∠ABD = ∠CBE (common angle) ∴ ΔABD ~ ΔCBE (by AA similarity criterion) (iii) In ΔAEP and ΔADB: ∠AEP = ∠ADB = 90° (altitudes) ∠PAE = ∠DAB (common angle) ∴ ΔAEP ~ ΔADB (by AA similarity criterion) Hence Proved.
34 5 Marks

From the top of a 100 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships on opposite sides of it are 30° and 45° respectively. Find the distance between the two ships. (Use √3 = 1.732)

✅ Solution:
Let AB be the lighthouse = 100 m Let ships be at C and D on opposite sides. Angle of depression to C = 30° ⇒ ∠ACB = 30° Angle of depression to D = 45° ⇒ ∠ADB = 45° In right ΔABC: tan 30° = AB/BC 1/√3 = 100/BC BC = 100√3 = 100 × 1.732 = 173.2 m In right ΔABD: tan 45° = AB/BD 1 = 100/BD BD = 100 m Distance between ships = BC + BD = 173.2 + 100 = 273.2 m
35 5 Marks

A cone of maximum volume is carved out from a solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm. Find the volume and total surface area of the remaining solid.

✅ Solution:
For maximum volume cone inside hemisphere: Radius of cone = Radius of hemisphere = 7 cm Height of cone = Radius of hemisphere = 7 cm Volume of hemisphere = (2/3)πr³ = (2/3) × (22/7) × 343 = (2 × 22 × 49)/3 = 2156/3 = 718.67 cm³ Volume of cone = (1/3)πr²h = (1/3) × (22/7) × 49 × 7 = (22 × 49)/3 = 1078/3 = 359.33 cm³ Volume of remaining solid = 718.67 – 359.33 = 359.33 cm³ Slant height of cone l = √(r² + h²) = √(49 + 49) = √98 = 7√2 cm CSA of cone = πrl = (22/7) × 7 × 7√2 = 154√2 cm² CSA of hemisphere = 2πr² = 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 308 cm² Total Surface Area = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere = 154√2 + 308 = 154(√2 + 2) = 154(1.414 + 2) = 154 × 3.414 = 525.76 cm²

🏏 Case Study: Cricket Tournament

A school is organizing an inter-house cricket tournament. The auditorium has seats arranged in rows. The first row has 20 seats, the second row has 24 seats, the third row has 28 seats, and so on. There are 15 rows in total.

(i) Find the number of seats in the 10th row.
(ii) Find the total number of seats in the auditorium.
(iii) If each ticket costs ₹50, find the total revenue if all seats are filled.

✅ Solution:
Seats form an AP: 20, 24, 28, ... a = 20, d = 4, n = 15 (i) Seats in 10th row: a₁₀ = a + 9d = 20 + 9(4) = 20 + 36 = 56 seats (ii) Total seats: S₁₅ = n/2 [2a + (n–1)d] = 15/2 [40 + 14(4)] = 15/2 [40 + 56] = 15/2 × 96 = 15 × 48 = 720 seats (iii) Total revenue = 720 × ₹50 = ₹36,000

🌳 Case Study: Tree Plantation Drive

A group of students decided to plant trees in and around their school to reduce air pollution. It was decided that the number of trees each section of each class will plant is equal to the class in which they are studying. For example, a section of Class I will plant 1 tree, a section of Class II will plant 2 trees, and so on till Class XII. There are three sections of each class.

(i) How many trees will be planted by the students of Class X?
(ii) Find the total number of trees planted by all sections of all classes.
(iii) What value is depicted by the students?

✅ Solution:
(i) Trees planted by Class X: Each section plants 10 trees. 3 sections × 10 trees = 30 trees (ii) Total trees by all classes: Class I: 3 × 1 = 3 Class II: 3 × 2 = 6 ... Class XII: 3 × 12 = 36 Total = 3(1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 12) = 3 × 12/2 × (1 + 12) = 3 × 6 × 13 = 234 trees (iii) Values depicted: • Environmental awareness • Social responsibility • Team work and cooperation • Care for nature and sustainable development

📊 Case Study: Mobile Phone Usage Survey

A survey was conducted in a school to find the time (in hours) spent by students on mobile phones daily. The data collected from 100 students is as follows:

Time (hours): 0-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-8, 8-10
No. of students: 15, 25, 30, 20, 10

(i) Find the modal class.
(ii) Find the median class.
(iii) Calculate the mean time spent by students on mobile phones.

✅ Solution:
(i) Modal class: Class with maximum frequency = 4-6 hours (frequency = 30) (ii) For median: n = 100, n/2 = 50 Cumulative frequencies: 15, 40, 70, 90, 100 50 lies in cumulative frequency 70. ∴ Median class = 4-6 hours (iii) Mean calculation: Class marks (x): 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 fx: 15, 75, 150, 140, 90 Σf = 100, Σfx = 470 Mean = Σfx/Σf = 470/100 = 4.7 hours
