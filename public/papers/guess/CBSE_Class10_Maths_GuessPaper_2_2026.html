1 1 Mark

The decimal expansion of 13/125 will terminate after how many decimal places?

  • (a) 1
  • (b) 2
  • (c) 3
  • (d) 4
✅ Answer: (c) 3
13/125 = 13/5³ = (13 × 2³)/(5³ × 2³) = 104/1000 = 0.104 Denominator = 10³, so terminates after 3 decimal places.
2 1 Mark

If α and β are zeroes of x² – 5x + 6, then α² + β² = ?

  • (a) 13
  • (b) 25
  • (c) 37
  • (d) 11
✅ Answer: (a) 13
α + β = 5, αβ = 6 α² + β² = (α+β)² – 2αβ = 25 – 12 = 13
3 1 Mark

The pair of equations x + 2y = 5 and 3x + ky = 15 has infinitely many solutions if k = ?

  • (a) 3
  • (b) 5
  • (c) 6
  • (d) 10
✅ Answer: (c) 6
For infinitely many solutions: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ 1/3 = 2/k = 5/15 1/3 = 2/k ⇒ k = 6
4 1 Mark

The roots of x² – 3x – 10 = 0 are:

  • (a) 2, 5
  • (b) –2, 5
  • (c) 2, –5
  • (d) –2, –5
✅ Answer: (b) –2, 5
x² – 3x – 10 = 0 x² – 5x + 2x – 10 = 0 x(x–5) + 2(x–5) = 0 (x+2)(x–5) = 0 x = –2 or x = 5

In ΔABC, if DE || BC, AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and AE = 5 cm, then EC = ?

8 1 Mark

If tan θ = 4/3, then (sin θ + cos θ)/(sin θ – cos θ) = ?

  • (a) 7
  • (b) –7
  • (c) 1/7
  • (d) –1/7
✅ Answer: (a) 7
tan θ = 4/3 ⇒ sin θ = 4/5, cos θ = 3/5 (sin θ + cos θ)/(sin θ – cos θ) = (4/5 + 3/5)/(4/5 – 3/5) = (7/5)/(1/5) = 7

From a point P, 13 cm from the centre of a circle, the length of tangent is 12 cm. The radius of the circle is:

  • (a) 3 cm
  • (b) 4 cm
  • (c) 5 cm
  • (d) 6 cm
✅ Answer: (c) 5 cm
OP² = OT² + PT² (tangent ⊥ radius) 13² = r² + 12² 169 = r² + 144 r² = 25 ⇒ r = 5 cm

If the mode of the data 12, 15, 18, 12, x, 15, 12, 18 is 12, then x = ?

  • (a) 12
  • (b) 15
  • (c) 18
  • (d) Any value

  • (b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT correct explanation of A

If the point (x, 4) lies on the line segment joining (–2, 1) and (4, 7), then x = ?

25 2 Marks

Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

✅ Solution:
Let 3 + 2√5 be rational. Then 3 + 2√5 = a/b where a, b are co-prime integers. 2√5 = a/b – 3 = (a – 3b)/b √5 = (a – 3b)/2b RHS is rational but LHS (√5) is irrational. This is a contradiction. ∴ 3 + 2√5 is irrational. (Proved)

Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are Nuri and Sonu?

✅ Solution:
Let present age of Nuri = x years Let present age of Sonu = y years 5 years ago: x – 5 = 3(y – 5) x – 5 = 3y – 15 x – 3y = –10 ... (i) 10 years later: x + 10 = 2(y + 10) x + 10 = 2y + 20 x – 2y = 10 ... (ii) Subtract (i) from (ii): y = 20 From (ii): x – 40 = 10 ⇒ x = 50 ∴ Nuri = 50 years, Sonu = 20 years
28 3 Marks

Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

✅ Solution:
Given: A circle with centre O. P is an external point. PA and PB are tangents. To Prove: PA = PB Construction: Join OA, OB and OP. Proof: In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: OA = OB (radii of same circle) ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius) OP = OP (common) ∴ ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (by RHS congruence rule) ∴ PA = PB (CPCT) Hence Proved.

Prove that: (sin θ + cosec θ)² + (cos θ + sec θ)² = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ

✅ Solution:
LHS = (sin θ + 1/sin θ)² + (cos θ + 1/cos θ)² = [(sin²θ + 1)/sin θ]² + [(cos²θ + 1)/cos θ]² = (sin²θ + 1)²/sin²θ + (cos²θ + 1)²/cos²θ = (sin⁴θ + 2sin²θ + 1)/sin²θ + (cos⁴θ + 2cos²θ + 1)/cos²θ = sin²θ + 2 + 1/sin²θ + cos²θ + 2 + 1/cos²θ = (sin²θ + cos²θ) + 4 + cosec²θ + sec²θ = 1 + 4 + (1 + cot²θ) + (1 + tan²θ) = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ = RHS Hence Proved.

A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less for the same journey. Find the speed of the train.

✅ Solution:
Let speed of train = x km/h Time taken = 360/x hours New speed = (x + 5) km/h New time = 360/(x+5) hours Given: 360/x – 360/(x+5) = 1 360[(x+5 – x)/x(x+5)] = 1 360 × 5 / x(x+5) = 1 1800 = x² + 5x x² + 5x – 1800 = 0 x² + 45x – 40x – 1800 = 0 x(x + 45) – 40(x + 45) = 0 (x – 40)(x + 45) = 0 x = 40 or x = –45 (rejected) ∴ Speed of train = 40 km/h
33 5 Marks

State and prove Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT). In ΔABC, DE || BC where D and E are points on AB and AC respectively. If AD = x, DB = x – 2, AE = x + 2 and EC = x – 1, find the value of x.

✅ Solution:
Statement of BPT: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle intersecting the other two sides, then it divides the two sides in the same ratio. Proof: Given: In ΔABC, DE || BC To Prove: AD/DB = AE/EC Construction: Join BE and CD. Draw EF ⊥ AB and DG ⊥ AC. Area(ΔADE)/Area(ΔBDE) = (½×AD×EF)/(½×DB×EF) = AD/DB Area(ΔADE)/Area(ΔCDE) = (½×AE×DG)/(½×EC×DG) = AE/EC Since ΔBDE and ΔCDE are on same base DE and between same parallels DE || BC: Area(ΔBDE) = Area(ΔCDE) ∴ AD/DB = AE/EC (Proved) Finding x: AD/DB = AE/EC x/(x–2) = (x+2)/(x–1) x(x–1) = (x+2)(x–2) x² – x = x² – 4 –x = –4 x = 4
34 5 Marks

The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of a tower is 30° and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building.

✅ Solution:
Let AB = building, CD = tower = 50 m Let distance between them = BD = x Let height of building AB = h In right ΔCDB: tan 60° = CD/BD √3 = 50/x x = 50/√3 m In right ΔABD: tan 30° = AB/BD 1/√3 = h/x h = x/√3 = (50/√3)/√3 = 50/3 = 16.67 m ∴ Height of building = 50/3 m or 16.67 m

A solid toy is in the form of a hemisphere surmounted by a right circular cone. The height of the cone is 4 cm and the diameter of the base is 6 cm. Determine the volume and total surface area of the toy. (Use π = 22/7)

✅ Solution:
Radius r = 6/2 = 3 cm Height of cone h = 4 cm Volume of toy = Volume of cone + Volume of hemisphere = (1/3)πr²h + (2/3)πr³ = (1/3)πr²(h + 2r) = (1/3) × (22/7) × 9 × (4 + 6) = (1/3) × (22/7) × 9 × 10 = (22 × 3 × 10)/7 = 660/7 = 94.29 cm³ Slant height of cone l = √(r² + h²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 cm TSA = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere = πrl + 2πr² = πr(l + 2r) = (22/7) × 3 × (5 + 6) = (22/7) × 3 × 11 = 726/7 = 103.71 cm²

🏫 Case Study: School Auditorium

A school auditorium has seats arranged in rows forming an arithmetic progression. The number of seats in the first 5 rows are 20, 23, 26, 29, and 32 respectively. The total number of rows in the auditorium is 20.

(i) Find the number of seats in the 15th row.
(ii) Find the total number of seats in the auditorium.
(iii) If each ticket costs ₹75, calculate the total amount collected if all seats are occupied.

✅ Solution:
a = 20, d = 3, n = 20 (i) Seats in 15th row: a₁₅ = a + 14d = 20 + 14(3) = 20 + 42 = 62 seats (ii) Total seats: S₂₀ = 20/2 [2(20) + 19(3)] = 10 [40 + 57] = 10 × 97 = 970 seats (iii) Total amount = 970 × ₹75 = ₹72,750

🏗️ Case Study: Construction Site

A construction company is building a flyover. They need to place two pillars on opposite sides of a 100 m wide road. From the top of the first pillar (height 60 m), the angle of depression of the base of the second pillar is 30°. From the top of the second pillar, the angle of depression of the base of the first pillar is 45°.

(i) Find the height of the second pillar.
(ii) Find the distance between the tops of the two pillars.
(iii) If the cost of construction is ₹5000 per meter height, find the total cost of both pillars.

✅ Solution:
Let first pillar AB = 60 m, second pillar CD = h m Distance between pillars BD = 100 m (i) From top A, angle of depression to D = 30° In right ΔABD (considering horizontal): tan 30° = AB/BD ... wait, let's reconsider. Actually, from top of first pillar (60m), angle of depression to base of second: If both pillars are on opposite sides of 100m road: Let horizontal distance = 100 m tan 30° = 60/100 ... this doesn't match. Correct approach: Let second pillar height = h From top of first (60m), depression angle 30° to base of second: tan 30° = 60/100 = 0.577 ... but tan 30° = 0.577 ✓ This confirms horizontal distance = 100 m. From top of second pillar, depression angle 45° to base of first: tan 45° = h/100 1 = h/100 h = 100 m (ii) Vertical difference = 100 – 60 = 40 m Horizontal distance = 100 m Distance between tops = √(40² + 100²) = √(1600 + 10000) = √11600 = 20√29 ≈ 107.7 m (iii) Total cost = (60 + 100) × 5000 = 160 × 5000 = ₹8,00,000

📱 Case Study: Social Media Usage

A survey was conducted among 80 students of Class 10 to find the time spent on social media daily. The data is as follows:

Time (hours): 0-1, 1-2, 2-3, 3-4, 4-5
No. of students: 8, 18, 24, 20, 10

(i) Find the median time spent by students on social media.
(ii) Find the mean time spent.
(iii) What advice would you give to students based on this data?

✅ Solution:
(i) Median: n = 80, n/2 = 40 Cumulative frequencies: 8, 26, 50, 70, 80 40 lies in cumulative frequency 50. Median class = 2-3 hours l = 2, cf = 26, f = 24, h = 1 Median = l + [(n/2 – cf)/f] × h = 2 + [(40 – 26)/24] × 1 = 2 + 14/24 = 2 + 0.583 = 2.583 hours ≈ 2 hours 35 minutes (ii) Mean: Class marks: 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5 fx: 4, 27, 60, 70, 45 Σf = 80, Σfx = 206 Mean = 206/80 = 2.575 hours ≈ 2 hours 35 minutes (iii) Advice: • Students should limit social media usage to 1-2 hours daily • More time should be spent on studies and physical activities • Digital detox is important for mental health • Balance between online and offline activities is essential
