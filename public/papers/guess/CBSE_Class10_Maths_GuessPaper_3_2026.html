A school is preparing for its annual drama competition. The stage has seats arranged in rows. The first row has 25 seats, the second row has 28 seats, the third row has 31 seats, and so on. There are 12 rows in total.

(i) Find the number of seats in the last row.
(ii) Find the total number of seats in the auditorium.
(iii) If 5 seats are reserved for judges and staff, how many seats are available for audience?

✅ Solution:
a = 25, d = 3, n = 12 (i) Seats in last (12th) row: a₁₂ = a + 11d = 25 + 11(3) = 25 + 33 = 58 seats (ii) Total seats: S₁₂ = 12/2 [2(25) + 11(3)] = 6 [50 + 33] = 6 × 83 = 498 seats (iii) Available seats = 498 – 5 = 493 seats

📚 Case Study: Library Books

A school librarian conducted a survey to find the number of books read by students in a month. The data collected from 50 students is as follows:

No. of books: 0-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-8, 8-10
No. of students: 6, 12, 18, 10, 4

(i) Find the median number of books read.
(ii) Find the mean number of books read.
(iii) What conclusion can you draw about the reading habits of students?

✅ Solution:
(i) Median: n = 50, n/2 = 25 Cumulative frequencies: 6, 18, 36, 46, 50 25 lies in cumulative frequency 36. Median class = 4-6 l = 4, cf = 18, f = 18, h = 2 Median = 4 + [(25–18)/18] × 2 = 4 + (7/18) × 2 = 4 + 14/18 = 4 + 0.778 = 4.778 ≈ 4.78 books (ii) Mean: Class marks: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 fx: 6, 36, 90, 70, 36 Σf = 50, Σfx = 238 Mean = 238/50 = 4.76 books (iii) Conclusion: • Most students read 4-6 books per month • Reading habits are moderate • Only a few students (4 out of 50) read more than 8 books • Students should be encouraged to read more books
