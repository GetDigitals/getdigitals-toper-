Discursive Passage (10 marks)
Education is not the filling of a pail, but the lighting of a fire. This profound statement by William Butler Yeats captures the true essence of learning. In our rapidly evolving world, the ability to learn, unlearn, and relearn has become more valuable than any specific set of facts. The jobs that exist today may not exist tomorrow, and the jobs of tomorrow haven't even been invented yet. Critical thinking is the cornerstone of modern education. It is the ability to analyse information objectively, question assumptions, and make reasoned judgments. A student who memorises formulas without understanding their application is like a chef who knows recipes but cannot taste the food. True learning happens when students connect knowledge to real-world situations. Collaborative learning has also gained prominence. When students work together, they learn to communicate effectively, respect diverse perspectives, and develop empathy. Research indicates that students retain 90% of what they teach others, compared to only 5% of what they hear in lectures. However, the greatest challenge facing education today is the digital divide. While some students have access to high-speed internet, online libraries, and virtual laboratories, others struggle with basic connectivity. Bridging this gap is essential for creating an equitable society where every child has the opportunity to succeed.
Q1(a)1 mark
What does Yeats mean by "education is the lighting of a fire"?
Answer: Yeats means true education should ignite curiosity and passion for learning, not just fill minds with facts. It should inspire students to explore, question, and discover on their own.Sachchi education bachchon ke andar curiosity aur passion jagati hai, sirf facts bharne se kaam nahi chalta.
Q1(b)1 mark
Why is the ability to "learn, unlearn, and relearn" important today?
Answer: Because the world is rapidly changing — today's jobs may disappear, new jobs will emerge. Students need to adapt continuously.Duniya tezi se badal rahi hai — isliye adapt karna aur naye skills seekhna zaroori hai.

Answer: A student who memorises without understanding is like a chef who knows recipes but cannot taste the food — both lack real practical understanding.Jaise chef recipe jaanta hai par taste nahi kar sakta, waise hi student formula yaad hai par apply nahi kar sakta.

Answer: The digital divide — the gap between students with technology access and those without.Digital divide — kuch students ke paas technology hai, kuch ke paas nahi.

Writing Skills & Grammar — 20 marks
Grammar (10 marks)
Q3(a)5 marks
Fill in the blanks: The Internet __(i)__ (has/have) revolutionised the way we communicate. It __(ii)__ (connect/connects) people from all over the world __(iii)__ (in/within) seconds. However, we __(iv)__ (must/should) use it wisely __(v)__ (because/so) it can also be misused.
Answer: (i) has (ii) connects (iii) in (iv) must (v) becauseInternet singular hai toh 'has'/'connects' aayega. 'Must' obligation, 'because' reason dene ke liye.
Q3(b)5 marks
Change into indirect speech: (a) Ram said, "I am going to the market." (b) She said to me, "Have you finished your homework?" (c) The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun." (d) He said, "I will help you tomorrow." (e) My mother said, "Don't waste your time."
Answer: (a) Ram said that he was going to the market. (b) She asked me if I had finished my homework. (c) The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun. (universal truth — no backshift) (d) He said that he would help me the next day. (e) My mother told me not to waste my time.Reported speech: present → past; tomorrow → next day; universal truth mein tense nahi badalta; imperative → told/ordered + to/not to.

Q55 marks
Pie chart shows a student's daily time (24 hours): School: 7 hrs | Sleep: 8 hrs | Self-Study: 4 hrs | Entertainment: 3 hrs | Others: 2 hrs. Write an analytical paragraph (100-120 words).
Answer: The given pie chart illustrates how a student distributes 24 hours daily. Sleep occupies the largest portion at 8 hours (33.3%), essential for health. School takes 7 hours (29.2%). Self-study accounts for 4 hours (16.7%). Entertainment consumes 3 hours (12.5%), while 2 hours (8.3%) go to other activities. The distribution appears balanced, though reducing entertainment slightly and increasing self-study could improve academic performance further.Pie chart mein har section ka percentage aur time mention karo, highest/lowest batao, conclusion mein suggestion do.

Q6(a)5 marks
(i) Who is the speaker, and who is he addressing? (ii) What is the speaker's purpose in narrating this family history? (iii) What is significant about the mention of 'Oxen Meadows'? (iv) What does this extract reveal about Lomov's manner of speaking? (v) What happens immediately after this speech, once Lomov mentions Oxen Meadows?
Answer: (i) The speaker is Lomov, addressing Natalya Stepanovna. (ii) He is building up to a marriage proposal, using their families' long friendship as a comfortable lead-in. (iii) Oxen Meadows becomes the trigger for the play's central comic conflict — Natalya immediately claims it belongs to her family, not Lomov, sparking a heated, absurd quarrel. (iv) It reveals Lomov as nervous, long-winded, and overly formal — he takes an unnecessarily roundabout path just to make a simple proposal. (v) Natalya interrupts and insists the Oxen Meadows are hers, leading to a bitter, prolonged argument that derails the proposal entirely — Chekhov's satire on how trivial disputes overshadow important matters.Lomov Natalya ko propose karne aaya hai par family history se baat shuru karta hai. Oxen Meadows ka zikr hote hi Natalya use apni zameen bata deti hai aur jhagda shuru ho jaata hai — yehi play ka comic conflict hai.
Q6(b)5 marks
(i) What is 'dust of snow'? (ii) Why does the poet mention a crow and a hemlock tree? (iii) What effect did the dust of snow have on the poet? (iv) What message does this poem convey? (v) Comment on the poet's choice of a crow (rather than a more 'beautiful' bird) for this moment of change.
Answer: (i) The fine particles of snow that fell from the tree like dust. (ii) Crow and hemlock are commonly seen as symbols of sadness/gloom (crow = bad omen, hemlock = poisonous tree) — the poet deliberately uses these negative symbols to show that even unpleasant things can bring joy. (iii) It changed the poet's mood from sad/rueful to happy, saving part of his day. (iv) Small, unexpected moments in nature can bring great happiness and shift our perspective. (v) By choosing a crow rather than a conventionally beautiful bird, Frost emphasises that joy and grace can come from the most ordinary or even 'unlikeable' sources — beauty isn't a prerequisite for meaning.Crow aur hemlock negative symbols hain, par poet dikhata hai ki inse bhi khushi mil sakti hai. Chhoti moments mood badal sakti hain.

How did the baker (pader) become a part of Goan culture and tradition? (40-50 words)

His bamboo staff's jingling sound was familiar to all, and his single-piece long frock (kabai) made him easily recognisable and beloved.Baker Goa ki culture ka hissa tha kyunki har occasion pe uski bread chahiye hoti thi. Long Answer Question — First Flight (6 marks)
Attempt any 1 out of the following 2 questions. 100-120 words. 6 marks.
Q9(a)6 marks
Describe Valli's first bus journey. What did she learn from it about life and death? (100-120 words)
Answer: Valli, an eight-year-old fascinated by the bus passing her village, saved her money, planned carefully, and took the bus to town alone. She enjoyed the landscape, palm trees, and fields on the way. On her return, she saw a dead cow on the road — the same cow that had amused her by running in front of the bus earlier. This sight deeply disturbed her, teaching her that life and death are part of nature; the cow that was alive and playful was now lifeless. This experience taught Valli that life is unpredictable and death inevitable. Her journey was not just a bus ride but one from innocence to maturity.Valli ne pehli baar bus journey ki. Return mein dead cow dekhkar usse life aur death ka matlab samajh aaya. Yeh uski innocence se maturity ki journey thi.
Q9(b)6 marks
Chekhov uses humour and exaggeration to comment on courtship in his times. Illustrate with examples from 'The Proposal'. (100-120 words)
Answer: Chekhov satirises the seriousness given to trivial matters over genuine emotional connection. Lomov's visit to propose to Natalya turns first into a bitter dispute over Oxen Meadows, and then a heated argument about whose dog — Squeezer or Guess — is superior. Both quarrels are utterly disproportionate to their real importance, yet consume the entire visit, delaying and nearly derailing the proposal itself. The exaggerated physical symptoms Lomov suffers from excitement (palpitations, numbness) add further comic absurdity. Through this, Chekhov mocks a society where landowning class courtship was more about property and pride than love — reducing marriage to a transaction wrapped in petty ego.Chekhov dikhata hai ki chhoti baaton (Oxen Meadows, kutte) pe zyada dhyaan diya jaata hai asli emotional connection se. Ye courtship ki triviality pe satire hai.

Q10(a)6 marks
How did a book of nursery rhymes save the Earth from the Martian invasion? What does this tell us about the power of simple wisdom? (100-120 words)
Answer: Think-Tank, the Martian commander, misinterpreted nursery rhymes as evidence of Earth's superior intelligence and military strength. "Humpty Dumpty had a great fall" convinced him Earthlings could break Martian eggs (their heads); "Hey Diddle Diddle" made him believe Earthlings had advanced space technology, with cows jumping over the moon. Terrified by these misreadings, Think-Tank ordered an immediate retreat, saving Earth. The story humorously shows that wisdom doesn't always come from complex knowledge — simple, innocent things can be more powerful than advanced technology when fear and misunderstanding are involved.Think-Tank ne nursery rhymes ko galat samajhkar dar gaya aur retreat kar liya. Story sikhati hai ki simple wisdom bhi powerful ho sakti hai.
Q10(b)6 marks
Discuss the character of Mrs. Pumphrey. How does the story use her relationship with Tricki to comment on human behaviour? (100-120 words)
Answer: Mrs. Pumphrey is a wealthy, well-meaning but excessively indulgent dog owner who equates love with overfeeding and pampering. Her constant treats and lack of exercise for Tricki make him dangerously obese and lethargic. When Dr. Herriot places Tricki on a strict regime — starvation, discipline, exercise — he transforms into a healthy, happy dog, ironically becoming greedy for food from other dogs' bowls at the surgery. The story uses Mrs. Pumphrey to satirise how misguided affection, when it avoids necessary discipline, can harm rather than help — a lesson equally applicable to parenting and any close relationship.Mrs. Pumphrey pyar ko overfeeding samajhti hai jisse Tricki bimaar ho jaata hai. Story dikhati hai ki discipline ke bina pyar nuksaan pahuncha sakta hai.
