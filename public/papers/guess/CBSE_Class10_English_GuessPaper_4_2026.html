Discursive Passage (10 marks)
Time management is not about doing more things in less time; it is about doing the right things at the right time. In our fast-paced world, where distractions are just a notification away, the ability to prioritise has become a superpower. Students who master time management not only achieve better grades but also experience less stress and more free time. The first step in effective time management is setting clear goals. Without a destination, any road will do — but it may not lead where you want to go. Breaking large tasks into smaller, manageable chunks makes them less overwhelming. The famous "Pomodoro Technique" suggests working for 25 minutes and then taking a 5-minute break, which has been proven to enhance focus and productivity. Procrastination is the thief of time. We often delay important tasks because they seem difficult or boring. The "two-minute rule" is helpful here: if a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately. Equally important is the ability to say "no." Every "yes" to something unimportant is a "no" to something that matters. Learning to decline unnecessary commitments frees up time for what truly counts — family, health, personal growth, and meaningful work.
Q1(a)1 mark
What is the true meaning of time management according to the passage?
Answer: Not about doing more things in less time, but about doing the right things at the right time.Time management sahi cheez sahi time pe karna hai, zyada cheezein kam time mein nahi.
Q1(b)1 mark
Why is the first step of goal-setting compared to having a 'destination'?
Answer: Because without clear goals (a destination), effort (any road) may not lead anywhere useful — direction matters more than mere activity.Bina clear goal ke, mehnat bhi galat direction mein ja sakti hai.
Q1(c)1 mark
What does the Pomodoro Technique suggest?
Answer: Working for 25 minutes followed by a 5-minute break, proven to enhance focus and productivity.25 minute kaam, 5 minute break — focus aur productivity badhata hai.
Q1(d)1 mark
What is the 'two-minute rule'?
Answer: If a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately, preventing small tasks from piling up.2 minute se kam wala kaam turant kar do, taaki chhote kaam jama na ho.
Q1(e)1 mark
Why is procrastination called 'the thief of time'?
Answer: Because we delay important tasks for easy, pleasurable activities, and the resulting anxiety of pending work ruins our leisure time too — stealing time both ways.Procrastination important kaam delay karwata hai aur baad mein anxiety leisure time bhi barbaad kar deta hai.
Q1(f)1 mark
Find words from the passage that mean the same as: (i) delaying (ii) overwhelming
Answer: (i) Procrastination (ii) 'Overwhelming' itself appears — a better matching pair: (i) delaying = Procrastination (ii) massive/priority = superpower's context word 'prioritise'Procrastination = delaying karna.
Q1(g)1 mark
What does the author mean by "every yes to something unimportant is a no to something that matters"?
Answer: Time is limited — agreeing to unimportant commitments necessarily takes time away from things that truly matter, like family, health, and meaningful work.Time limited hai — chhoti cheezon ko haan bolna important cheezon ko na bolna hai.
Q1(h)1 mark

Answer: "The Art of Managing Time" OR "Doing the Right Things at the Right Time""The Art of Managing Time" ya "Doing the Right Things at the Right Time".
Q1(i)1 mark
How does breaking large tasks into smaller chunks help, according to the passage?
Answer: It makes large, overwhelming projects feel achievable by turning them into daily, manageable targets.Bade tasks chhote daily targets mein todne se woh achievable lagte hain.
Q1(j)1 mark
What is the overall tone of the passage — motivational, critical, or neutral? Justify.
Answer: The tone is motivational and practical. The author offers actionable techniques (Pomodoro, two-minute rule) rather than merely criticising poor time management, encouraging readers to improve.Tone motivational hai — author practical techniques deta hai, sirf criticism nahi karta.

Why did Lencho write a letter to God? What does this show about his character? (40-50 words)

This shows his unshakeable faith in God and his simplicity.Lencho ne God ko letter likha kyunki hailstorm ne fasal barbaad kar di. Yeh uska God pe blind faith dikhata hai.
Q7(b)3 marks
What are Mandela's "twin obligations"? Why was it difficult for a black South African to fulfil them? (40-50 words)
Answer: Obligations towards family, and towards his people/country. It was difficult because apartheid denied basic rights, and fighting oppression for the country put families in danger.Twin obligations = family aur country ke prati duty. Apartheid ki wajah se yeh mushkil tha.

How did Valli plan her first bus journey? What does this tell us about her? (40-50 words)

This shows she was intelligent, curious, determined, and resourceful for an eight-year-old.Valli ne har paise bachaya aur information collect ki. Yeh uski intelligence aur determination dikhata hai.
Q7(d)3 marks
What instructions does Amanda receive from the adult voice? How does she escape from reality? (40-50 words)

What are the two opinions about the end of the world mentioned in the poem? Which one does the poet favour? (40-50 words)

Q9(a)6 marks
Describe Anne's relationship with her diary. Why does she call it her 'true friend'? How does the diary help her cope with loneliness? (100-120 words)
Answer: Anne Frank's relationship with her diary was deeply personal. She named it "Kitty" and treated it as her closest confidant. In the cramped, fearful Secret Annex, the diary became her only outlet for expression. She called it her "true friend" because unlike people, it never judged her, never betrayed her secrets, and always listened patiently. She could share her deepest fears and dreams without fear of rejection. The diary helped her cope with loneliness by giving a sense of companionship, preserving her identity and sanity during the darkest days. Through writing, she processed her emotions and maintained hope — the diary was her voice, friend, and survival tool.Anne ne diary ko 'Kitty' naam diya. Diary ne usse loneliness mein support diya aur uski identity bachayi.
Q9(b)6 marks
"Faith in God can move mountains." Do you agree with this statement in the context of Lencho's story? (100-120 words)
Answer: Yes, Lencho's story illustrates unshakeable faith. When a hailstorm destroyed his crop, he didn't despair — he wrote directly to God for 100 pesos. Though post office employees collected only 70 pesos, Lencho's faith never wavered; he doubted the postal workers, never God. This teaches that true faith gives strength to face adversity, and his unwavering belief inspires readers that complete trust can provide courage to overcome life's biggest challenges, even when the outcome isn't exactly as expected.Lencho ki kahani sikhati hai ki sachcha vishwas mushkil se ladne ki taqat deta hai.
Long Answer Question — Footprints Without Feet (6 marks)
Attempt any 1 out of the following 2 questions. 100-120 words. 6 marks.
Q10(a)6 marks
How did Anil's kindness transform Hari Singh? What does this story teach us about trust and redemption? (100-120 words)
Answer: Hari Singh, a seasoned thief, came to rob Anil, but Anil's kindness, trust, and simple lifestyle gradually changed him. Anil taught him to cook, gave him pocket money, and promised to teach him to read. When Hari stole Anil's money and tried to flee, guilt overcame him — he realised Anil's trust was more valuable than the money, and returned it, welcomed back without punishment. This story teaches that kindness and trust can reform even hardened criminals; everyone deserves a second chance, and redemption is possible when someone believes in us.Anil ki kindness ne Hari ko badal diya. Trust aur forgiveness se ek thief bhi sudhar sakta hai.
Q10(b)6 marks
Discuss how Matilda's vanity leads to a decade of hardship. What lesson does the story teach about contentment? (100-120 words)
Answer: Matilda's obsession with appearing wealthy leads her to borrow a necklace for a party, which she then loses. To repay the debt, she and her husband work for ten gruelling years, losing their youth and comfort. The final irony — the necklace was fake, worth a fraction of what they paid — makes her sacrifice tragically pointless. The story teaches that contentment with what one has is a far surer path to happiness than chasing appearances; Matilda's decade of suffering stemmed entirely from a single dishonest moment of vanity, showing how small choices rooted in pride can have life-altering consequences.Matilda ki vanity ne uski zindagi barbaad kar di. Story sikhati hai ki contentment happiness ka asli raasta hai.
