Discursive Passage (10 marks)
The concept of sustainable living has moved from being an environmental buzzword to an urgent necessity. Our planet is facing unprecedented challenges — climate change, deforestation, pollution, and depletion of natural resources. The choices we make today will determine the world our children inherit tomorrow. Sustainable living is not about making drastic sacrifices; it is about making mindful choices. Simple actions like reducing plastic use, conserving water, using public transport, and recycling can collectively create a significant impact. A single reusable bag can replace hundreds of plastic bags over its lifetime. The food we eat also plays a crucial role. The meat industry is one of the largest contributors to greenhouse gas emissions. By reducing meat consumption and choosing locally grown, seasonal produce, we can significantly lower our carbon footprint. Food waste is another major concern — approximately one-third of all food produced globally goes to waste. Education is the key to spreading awareness about sustainability. When children learn about environmental conservation from an early age, they grow up to be responsible citizens. Schools that incorporate sustainability into their curriculum create a generation that thinks before consuming and acts before it is too late.

Q9(a)6 marks
Describe the quarrel between Lomov and Natalya over the Oxen Meadows. How does this quarrel highlight the theme of the play? (100-120 words)
Answer: Lomov came to propose to Natalya but instead got into a heated argument over the ownership of Oxen Meadows. Lomov claimed the meadows were his; Natalya insisted they belonged to her family. The argument grew so intense that Lomov's palpitations returned and he nearly fainted. They used abusive language and refused to listen to each other. This quarrel is central to the play's theme — it satirises the petty quarrels of the landed gentry who fight over trivial matters while pretending to be civilised. The irony is that they argue about property while nearly destroying the very proposal that brought them together. Chekhov uses this comedy to show human nature is fundamentally irrational, and love often gets lost in ego and property disputes.Lomov aur Natalya Oxen Meadows pe ladne lage jabki Lomov propose karne aaya tha. Yeh play ka main theme hai — petty quarrels aur ego.
Q9(b)6 marks
Explain the idea that 'the oppressor is also unfree'. How does Mandela develop this idea in the text? (100-120 words)
Answer: Mandela argues that both the oppressed and the oppressor are robbed of their humanity. The oppressed lose their freedom through discrimination and violence; the oppressor, meanwhile, is imprisoned by hatred, fear, and narrow-mindedness — he too is not free to express his humanity fully, trapped by the prejudice that drives his cruelty. Mandela develops this radical idea by describing how both the man who takes freedom and the man whose freedom is taken are diminished. This is why Mandela insists true liberation must free everyone, including the oppressors, from the system of hatred — turning the freedom struggle into a moral mission for all South Africans, not just revenge for one group.Mandela kehte hain ki oppressor bhi apni hatred ki wajah se free nahi hai. Isliye freedom struggle sabke liye hona chahiye, sirf revenge ke liye nahi.

Q10(a)6 marks
How did Griffin misuse his scientific discovery? What does his story tell us about the relationship between science and morality? (100-120 words)
Answer: Griffin discovered a drug making the human body invisible, but instead of using this for humanity's benefit, he misused it for theft, arson, and chaos — robbing a clergyman, stealing from a shop, and attacking his landlord. Griffin's story reminds us that scientific knowledge without moral values is dangerous. Science is a tool that can heal or harm, build or destroy. Griffin had brilliant scientific ability but lacked conscience and ethics — his downfall was inevitable because he used genius for selfish, criminal purposes. This teaches us that scientific progress must always be guided by ethics, and every scientist has a responsibility to use knowledge for society's greater good, not personal gain.Griffin ne invisibility ka misuse kiya. Story sikhati hai ki science bina morality ke dangerous hai.
Q10(b)6 marks
Richard Ebright's success shows that curiosity and mentorship matter more than natural genius. Discuss with reference to the chapter. (100-120 words)
Answer: Richard Ebright's journey began not with extraordinary genius but with simple childhood curiosity — collecting butterflies and studying insects. His mother nurtured this curiosity by buying him books, a microscope, and encouraging his questions rather than giving direct answers. Teachers and mentors like Dr. Urquhart guided his growing interest into rigorous scientific method. His success in identifying the monarch butterfly's diapause hormone came from years of patient observation and persistence, not innate brilliance alone. This shows that scientific greatness is built through nurtured curiosity, supportive mentorship, and sustained effort — qualities available to any dedicated student, not a rare gift reserved for a few.Richard ki safalta sirf genius se nahi, curiosity aur mother ke support se aayi. Yeh dikhata hai ki hard work aur mentorship zaroori hai.
