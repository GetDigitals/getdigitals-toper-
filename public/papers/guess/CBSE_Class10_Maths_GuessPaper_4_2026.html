1 1 Mark

The decimal expansion of 23/(2³ × 5²) will terminate after:

  • (a) 2 places
  • (b) 3 places
  • (c) 4 places
  • (d) 5 places
✅ Answer: (b) 3 places
Denominator = 2³ × 5² For terminating decimal, we need equal powers of 2 and 5. Multiply by 5¹: 2³ × 5³ = 10³ ∴ Terminates after 3 decimal places.
2 1 Mark

If α and β are zeroes of x² + 5x + 6, then the polynomial whose zeroes are 1/α and 1/β is:

  • (a) 6x² + 5x + 1
  • (b) 6x² – 5x + 1
  • (c) x² + 5x + 6
  • (d) x² – 5x + 6
✅ Answer: (a) 6x² + 5x + 1
α + β = –5, αβ = 6 New sum = 1/α + 1/β = (α+β)/αβ = –5/6 New product = 1/αβ = 1/6 Polynomial = x² – (–5/6)x + 1/6 = x² + (5/6)x + 1/6 = (6x² + 5x + 1)/6 ∴ Polynomial = 6x² + 5x + 1

The pair of equations 3x + 2y = 5 and 2x – 3y = 7 has:

  • (a) One solution

  • (c) No solution

✅ Answer: (a) One solution
a₁/a₂ = 3/2 b₁/b₂ = 2/(–3) = –2/3 Since a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂, lines intersect at one point. ∴ Unique solution.

14 1 Mark

Assertion (A): The number 6ⁿ can never end with digit 0 for any natural number n.
Reason (R): The prime factorization of 6ⁿ contains only 2 and 5.

  • (a) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation of A
  • (b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT correct explanation of A
  • (c) A is true but R is false
  • (d) A is false but R is true
✅ Answer: (c) A is true but R is false
6ⁿ = (2×3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ For number to end with 0, it needs both 2 and 5 in prime factorization. 6ⁿ has 2 and 3, but NOT 5. ∴ 6ⁿ can never end with 0. A is TRUE. But R says prime factorization contains only 2 and 5 — this is FALSE because it contains 3, not 5. ∴ A is true but R is false.

Prove that √2 + √3 is irrational.

Find all the zeroes of 2x⁴ – 3x³ – 3x² + 6x – 2, if two of its zeroes are √2 and –√2.

✅ Solution:
Since √2 and –√2 are zeroes, (x – √2)(x + √2) = x² – 2 is a factor. Dividing 2x⁴ – 3x³ – 3x² + 6x – 2 by x² – 2: Quotient = 2x² – 3x + 1 2x² – 3x + 1 = 0 2x² – 2x – x + 1 = 0 2x(x–1) – 1(x–1) = 0 (2x–1)(x–1) = 0 x = 1/2 or x = 1 ∴ All zeroes are: √2, –√2, 1, 1/2

Prove that the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
OR
AD is a median of ΔABC and AM ⊥ BC. Prove that:
(i) AC² = AD² + BC·DM + (BC/2)²
(ii) AB² = AD² – BC·DM + (BC/2)²

✅ Solution (Area Theorem):
Given: ΔABC ~ ΔPQR To Prove: ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔPQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)² Construction: Draw AM ⊥ BC and PN ⊥ QR. Proof: ar(ΔABC) = (1/2) × BC × AM ar(ΔPQR) = (1/2) × QR × PN ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔPQR) = (BC × AM)/(QR × PN) ... (iii) From (i), (ii), (iii): ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔPQR) = (BC/QR) × (AB/PQ) = (AB/PQ) × (AB/PQ) = (AB/PQ)² Similarly = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)² Hence Proved.

As observed from the top of a 75 m high lighthouse from the sea-level, the angles of depression of two ships are 30° and 45°. If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships. (Use √3 = 1.732)

✅ Solution:
Let AB = lighthouse = 75 m Let ships be at C and D (C nearer, D farther) In right ΔABC (for nearer ship at 45°): tan 45° = AB/BC 1 = 75/BC BC = 75 m In right ΔABD (for farther ship at 30°): tan 30° = AB/BD 1/√3 = 75/BD BD = 75√3 = 75 × 1.732 = 129.9 m Distance between ships = BD – BC = 129.9 – 75 = 54.9 m

A container shaped like a right circular cylinder having diameter 12 cm and height 15 cm is full of ice cream. The ice cream is to be filled into cones of height 12 cm and diameter 6 cm, having a hemispherical shape on the top. Find the number of such cones which can be filled with ice cream.

✅ Solution:

🏃 Case Study: Marathon Training

A coach is training students for a marathon. On the first day, a student runs 2 km. Each subsequent day, the student increases the running distance by 500 m. The training continues for 30 days.

(i) Find the distance run on the 15th day.
(ii) Find the total distance run in 30 days.
(iii) On which day will the student run 10 km?

✅ Solution:
a = 2 km = 2000 m, d = 500 m, n = 30 (i) Distance on 15th day: a₁₅ = a + 14d = 2000 + 14(500) = 2000 + 7000 = 9000 m = 9 km (ii) Total distance in 30 days: S₃₀ = 30/2 [2(2000) + 29(500)] = 15 [4000 + 14500] = 15 × 18500 = 277500 m = 277.5 km (iii) When distance = 10 km = 10000 m: aₙ = 10000 2000 + (n–1)500 = 10000 (n–1)500 = 8000 n–1 = 16 n = 17 ∴ On 17th day, the student will run 10 km.

🏠 Case Study: Housing Complex

A housing complex has two towers, Tower A and Tower B, standing on opposite sides of a 60 m wide road. From the top of Tower A (height 40 m), the angle of depression of the base of Tower B is 30°. From the top of Tower B, the angle of depression of the base of Tower A is 45°.

(i) Find the height of Tower B.
(ii) Find the difference in heights of the two towers.
(iii) If each floor is 4 m high, how many more floors does the taller tower have?

✅ Solution:
Let Tower A = 40 m, Tower B = h m Distance between towers = 60 m (i) From top of A (40m), depression 30° to base of B: tan 30° = 40/60 = 0.667 ≠ 1/√3 This doesn't work with 60m. Let me use standard interpretation where horizontal distance is calculated from angles. From top of A, depression 30°: Horizontal distance = 40/tan 30° = 40√3 ≈ 69.28 m From top of B, depression 45° to base of A: If horizontal distance is same = 69.28 m: tan 45° = h/69.28 1 = h/69.28 h = 69.28 m (ii) Difference = 69.28 – 40 = 29.28 m (iii) Extra floors = 29.28/4 ≈ 7.32 ≈ 7 floors Alternative standard interpretation with 60m road: From top of A (40m), depression 30° means the base of B is at horizontal distance: tan 30° = 40/x ⇒ x = 40√3 ≈ 69.28 m Since road is only 60m wide, the observation is not directly across. Let's assume the 60m is the horizontal distance: From A (40m), depression to base of B at 60m: tan θ = 40/60 = 2/3, θ ≈ 33.7° (not 30°) Given the question parameters, the most standard interpretation: Horizontal distance = 60 m From A (40m), depression to top of B or some point = 30° Actually, let's use: From A (40m), depression 30° to base of B at distance d: d = 40√3 From B (h), depression 45° to base of A at same distance: h = d = 40√3 ≈ 69.28 m The 60m might be irrelevant or approximate. I'll use calculated values. (i) h = 40√3 ≈ 69.28 m ≈ 69.3 m (ii) Difference = 40√3 – 40 = 40(√3 – 1) ≈ 29.28 m (iii) Extra floors = 40(√3–1)/4 = 10(√3–1) ≈ 7.32 ≈ 7 floors

🎮 Case Study: Gaming Hours

A survey was conducted among 60 students to find the number of hours they spend playing video games daily. The data is as follows:

Hours: 0-1, 1-2, 2-3, 3-4, 4-5
Students: 8, 14, 20, 12, 6

(i) Find the median time spent on gaming.
(ii) Find the mean time spent on gaming.
(iii) What health advice would you give to students based on this data?

✅ Solution:
(i) Median: n = 60, n/2 = 30 Cumulative frequencies: 8, 22, 42, 54, 60 30 lies in cumulative frequency 42. Median class = 2-3 l = 2, cf = 22, f = 20, h = 1 Median = 2 + [(30–22)/20] × 1 = 2 + 8/20 = 2 + 0.4 = 2.4 hours (ii) Mean: Class marks: 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5 fx: 4, 21, 50, 42, 27 Σf = 60, Σfx = 144 Mean = 144/60 = 2.4 hours (iii) Health Advice: • Limit gaming to 1-2 hours daily • Take regular breaks (20-20-20 rule: every 20 min, look 20 feet away for 20 sec) • Engage in physical activities and outdoor sports • Maintain proper posture while gaming • Ensure adequate sleep (7-8 hours)
